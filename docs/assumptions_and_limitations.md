# Assumptions and Limitations Register

**Status:** Phase 3 · **Last updated:** 2026-10-05

This register records every assumption, choice and limitation that could affect the project's results. It is added to phase by phase.

**Entry types:**
- **OBSERVED**: a fact about the data itself.
- **DERIVED**: a property of something we compute.
- **ASSUMPTION**: a modeling belief that can't be directly verified.
- **SCORING CHOICE**: a decision-model design choice.
- **LIMITATION**: a known weakness.

**Status values:**
- **Accepted**: in force.
- **Open**: a decision is pending.
- **To verify**: needs confirmation from data.
- **Closed**: resolved, with the resolution noted.

Source IDs (S-xx) and feature IDs (Fxx) refer to [data_sources.md](data_sources.md).

| A-001 | ASSUMPTION | The market universe is the top 35 U.S. MSAs by PEP Vintage 2025 population. It is fixed before scoring, with no additions for deployment activity. As a result, San Jose (41940, rank 36) and every smaller market are excluded. | A pre-committed, reproducible rule avoids cherry-picking and selection bias toward current deployment markets. | Markets below the cutoff can't appear in any recommendation. That includes San Jose, a known AV market, and many smaller markets where drones operate. The reasonableness check will show mismatches caused by this scope rather than by market fit. | 9 (validation); 12 (case study) | Accepted |
| A-002 | ASSUMPTION | The analytical market is the CBSA (Metropolitan Statistical Area), keyed by the 5-digit CBSA code (`market_id`), under the July 2023 OMB delineation. | CBSAs are official, built from counties, and published natively by Census and BLS. They also give a clean crosswalk for county-level data. | A CBSA does not match any operator's real service area. Early deployments cover small parts of a metro, and some metros share a single real-world market (for example, the Bay Area is split across the SF and San Jose CBSAs). | 5, 9 | Accepted |
| A-003 | LIMITATION | Sources come from different years. PEP 2025, ACS 2020–2024, CBP 2023, OEWS May 2025, FAA CY2025, EPA SLD (inputs from 2017–2020), climate normals for 1991–2020, and regulatory evidence dated 2026 (see data_sources.md §9). | Each source is pinned to its latest defensible release. Waiting for the years to line up isn't possible. | Features from older sources (especially the EPA built-environment data) may understate recent change in fast-growing metros. Comparisons across features mix years. | 5 (EDA flags), 12 (methodology) | Accepted |
| A-004 | ASSUMPTION | Every source is pinned to one vintage and frozen once Phase 3 ingestion begins. Newer releases (for example PEP Vintage 2026, ACS 2021–2025) are not adopted mid-project. | This keeps the pipeline reproducible and the rankings stable against data revisions. | Results may lag the newest data by the end of the project. | 12 | Accepted |
| A-005 | ASSUMPTION | ACS 2020–2024 and CBP 2023 are assumed to use the July 2023 delineation, inferred from their contents and reference dates. Neither publisher states it explicitly. | The CBP 2023 file contains 925 CBSAs (387 + 538), matching July 2023. The 2023 ACS products are documented as using Bulletin 23-01. | If this is wrong, some codes or county compositions would differ from the market list. | 3 (check that every market_id and name matches S-01) | **Verified (Phase 3, 2026-10-05).** For all 35 markets, the RAW tables for ACS 2020–2024, Gazetteer 2024 and BLS OEWS May 2025 carry the same CBSA codes and identical names as PEP (0 missing, 0 name differences). CBP 2023 contains all 35 codes (it carries no names). Every ACS table returns 935 CBSAs (387 + 538) |
| A-006 | ASSUMPTION | Sub-county sources (tracts, block groups) and the EPA SLD are assigned to CBSAs through **county FIPS** and the July 2023 delineation file. The SLD's own `CBSA` field, which uses a pre-2023 delineation, is never used. | Tracts and block groups nest exactly within counties, so the county join is reliable. The embedded field is out of date (for example, Dutchess County NY carries the old code 39100). | This is correct only if the counties of the 35 markets have unchanged FIPS codes across source years. Connecticut's planning regions don't affect any of the 35 markets. | 3 | **Verified (Phase 3, 2026-10-05).** The delineation file and PEP list the same 319 counties for the 35 markets. All 319 are present in Gazetteer 2024 counties and in the EPA SLD block groups (`STATEFP` + `COUNTYFP`). 11 of those counties carry a different (pre-2023) CBSA in the SLD's own field, which confirms that field must not be used |
| A-007 | ASSUMPTION | The climate normals at the chosen station(s) are assumed to represent a CBSA's operating climate. The station-to-CBSA rule is not decided yet. Options are a primary first-order airport station, a mean of in-CBSA stations, or a population-weighted nearest station. | Normals are published by station only. A single first-order station has complete elements plus hourly wind. | Large or climatically varied CBSAs (for example Riverside–San Bernardino and Denver) may be misrepresented. The choice can shift Operating Environment rankings. | 3 (rule chosen and documented); 8 (sensitivity) | Open |
| A-008 | LIMITATION | Metro-wide averages blur the difference between the urban core and the periphery. Raw MSA density is distorted by large, low-density outlying counties. | CBSAs include whole counties. Riverside–San Bernardino and Phoenix are examples where the county footprint is huge. | This could mis-rank markets on density-dependent features for robotaxis and sidewalk robots. The planned response is tract-level population-weighted density (a DERIVED measure). The formula is not chosen yet (data_sources.md §6). | 5, 6 | Open |
| A-009 | ASSUMPTION | Airport activity is assigned to a CBSA only for FAA commercial-service airports located physically inside it (point-in-polygon on NASR coordinates). | This is a documented, reproducible rule that doesn't depend on unreliable county-name or city fields. | Metros served by airports just outside their boundary are under-counted. Examples: BWI for DC, ONT for LA, MHT/PVD for Boston. A "nearby hub" sensitivity variable is proposed, with its distance threshold still undecided. | 3, 6 | Open |
| A-010 | LIMITATION | Enplanements at connecting hubs (for example ATL, DFW, CLT, DEN, ORD) include connecting passengers who never enter the local market. | FAA enplanement data doesn't separate local passengers from connecting ones. | Airport demand is overstated for hub metros, including when expressed per capita. | 5, 6 | Accepted |
| A-011 | LIMITATION | Regulatory evidence is qualitative, point-in-time and subject to frequent change. Recent examples are TX SB 2807 (2025), CA SB 480 (effective 2026-01-01), and the BVLOS rule, which is still only proposed. Local ordinances can't be listed comprehensively, and some official sources block automated capture (Texas and Georgia codes, NHTSA and FAA pages). | Regulation is set by statute and agency programs, not published as a dataset. | Classification errors or stale entries could misstate readiness. That is why every record carries a citation, an effective date, an access date and a verification status, and why no numeric score exists yet. | 3 (evidence capture), 7 (any ordinal translation), 11 (re-verification) | Accepted |
| A-012 | ASSUMPTION | For the 11 multi-state CBSAs, how state-level regulatory evidence maps to the market is undecided. Options are principal-city state, population-weighted state shares, or carrying every state with a divergence flag. | State law applies by state, but these markets cross state lines. State population shares are observed from the PEP county rows. | Balanced multi-state markets could be classified differently depending on the rule: Washington (VA 49% / MD 39% / DC 11%), Kansas City (MO 59% / KS 41%), New York (NY 65% / NJ 35%) and Philadelphia (PA 67% / NJ 22% / DE 9%). | 7 (rule chosen); 8 (sensitivity) | Open |
| A-013 | LIMITATION | The EPA National Walkability Index is a *relative* national ranking (1–20). Its inputs are from 2017–2020 on 2010-based block groups, and it doesn't measure sidewalks directly. | It is the only verified national, consistent built-environment index. | Walkability features show relative standing, not absolute sidewalk suitability, and may be out of date for fast-changing areas. | 5 | Accepted |
| A-014 | LIMITATION | Many candidate features are expected to rise with market size: population, households, establishments, enplanements, commuters. | Scale drives most count-based measures. | Size could be double-counted within or across components. Phase 5 must check correlation and redundancy, and prefer per-capita or density measures where appropriate. | 5, 6 | Open |
| A-015 | SCORING CHOICE | Current autonomous-deployment evidence is used **only as external validation**. It is never used for market selection, as a feature (see F52, dropped), or as a label. | Using deployments as an input would make the reasonableness check circular. Deployments also reflect company strategy and history, not only market fit. | The model can't reward existing ecosystems. The validation will show where public-data fit and actual company choices differ. | 9, 11 | Accepted |
| A-016 | SCORING CHOICE | No component weights, normalization methods or scoring formulas exist yet. When they are defined, they will be explicit decision-model assumptions and will be stress-tested through the sensitivity analysis. | Weights shouldn't be set before the data has been inspected. | None yet. Later, rankings will depend on these choices, which is why the sensitivity analysis is mandatory. | 7, 8 | Open |
| A-017 | LIMITATION | BLS OEWS wage data exclude the self-employed, so gig ride-hail and delivery drivers are largely missing. Small occupations may be suppressed. | OEWS is an establishment survey. | Labor-cost proxies (F25, F26) reflect employed drivers and couriers, not the platform gig workforce. | 3 (check suppression), 5 | Accepted |
| A-018 | LIMITATION | ACS 2020–2024 covers the pandemic years. Work-from-home and commute measures reflect a transitional period. | This is the latest 5-year release, and the 2021–2025 release is delayed. | Commute-based demand features (F05–F08) may understate typical travel demand, unevenly across metros. | 5 | Accepted |
| A-019 | ASSUMPTION | Counts of restaurants, food-service outlets and retail establishments (CBP) are assumed to proxy delivery demand opportunity. | There is no public data on delivery order volume. | Establishment counts don't reflect order volume, size or delivery adoption. CBP noise infusion adds small distortions. | 5, 6 | Accepted |
| A-020 | LIMITATION | The drone airspace features (UAS Facility Maps, security restrictions) show where controlled or restricted airspace is, not whether drones can actually operate there. | Controlled airspace is routinely authorized through LAANC and waivers. Part 135 operators work under exemptions. Temporary flight restrictions aren't included. | The airspace constraint could be overstated for airport-dense metros. These features are therefore marked PROXY. | 5, 6 | Accepted |
| A-021 | ASSUMPTION | In the climate normals, a missing snow value is treated as **missing, not zero**. A documented fallback rule will be chosen: the nearest in-CBSA station with complete flags, or NA plus a flag. | Some stations have no published snow normal (for example Los Angeles Intl and Phoenix Sky Harbor). Treating a blank as zero is an unverified assumption. | If handled inconsistently, the snow features for warm metros would rest on imputed values. | 4 (rule applied in dbt staging; Phase 3 landed the normals as published, blanks included) | Open |
| A-022 | SCORING CHOICE | The federal regulatory layers (FAA Part 107/135, BVLOS rulemaking status, NHTSA SGO / AV Framework) are recorded as context only. They are not differentiating features. | They apply identically to every market. | None on relative rankings. Documented so the methodology is complete. | 7 | Accepted |
| A-023 | LIMITATION | Public data can't observe proprietary economics, fleet costs, partnerships, safety performance, detailed road networks, local permitting relationships, or company strategy. | These are the limits of an independent analysis. | Rankings describe public-data market attractiveness, not operator-specific viability. A lower rank doesn't mean a market is unsuitable. | 12 (limitations page) | Accepted |
| A-024 | LIMITATION | Some Census products are delayed or suspended: CBP 2024 has no planned release date, and the 2025 ACS 1-year release date is undetermined, both because of a disclosure-avoidance order. The Census API now requires a key. | These are current publisher conditions (checked 2026-09-29). | They reinforce the pinned vintages (A-004). The key is needed before Phase 3 ingestion. | 2, 3 | Accepted |
| A-025 | OBSERVED | The populations used to select markets are PEP *estimates* for July 1, 2025. Rank 35 (Cleveland, 2,165,775) is about 181,000 people (about 8%) above rank 36 (San Jose, 1,984,473). | PEP is the official intercensal estimate series. | The selection is not sensitive to plausible estimation error at the cutoff. | — | Accepted |
| A-026 | LIMITATION | BLS OEWS May 2025 does not publish every occupation for every metro. For example, there is no 53-3054 (Taxi Drivers) row for New York (35620). Published wage cells can also hold the BLS markers `*` (estimate not available) or `#` (top-coded wage). In the MSA file the two median-wage columns together hold 11,005 `*` and 416 `#` cells. | Observed in the landed RAW data (Phase 3). BLS suppresses estimates that fail reliability or confidentiality standards. | Labor-cost features (F25, F26) may be missing for some market × occupation combinations. A missing value must not be read as a low wage. Markers must be handled explicitly in staging, and fallback occupations or documented NA handling are needed. | 4, 5 | Open |
| A-027 | LIMITATION | The hourly climate normals (S-13) were landed with **wind columns only**: identifiers plus every `HLY-WIND-*` column and its flags, 38 of 113 source columns, all 4,090,920 rows. The other hourly variables (temperature, dew point, pressure, cloud, heat index, wind chill) are not in RAW. | Only wind is needed by an approved candidate feature (F19); landing everything would roughly triple the volume for no current use. | Any future feature needing other hourly variables requires a new, separately audited ingestion run, not a re-cut of the existing table. | 5, 6 | Accepted |
