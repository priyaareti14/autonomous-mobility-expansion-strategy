# Assumptions and Limitations Register

**Status:** Phase 5 (in review) · **Last updated:** 2026-10-05

This register records every assumption, choice and limitation that could affect the project's results. It is added to phase by phase.

**Entry types:**
- **OBSERVED**: a fact about the data itself.
- **DERIVED**: a property of something we compute.
- **ASSUMPTION**: a modeling belief that can't be directly verified.
- **SCORING CHOICE**: a decision-model design choice.
- **LIMITATION**: a known weakness.

**Status values:**
- **Accepted**: in force.
- **Open**: a decision is pending.
- **To verify**: needs confirmation from data.
- **Closed**: resolved, with the resolution noted.

Source IDs (S-xx) and feature IDs (Fxx) refer to [data_sources.md](data_sources.md).

| A-001 | ASSUMPTION | The market universe is the top 35 U.S. MSAs by PEP Vintage 2025 population. It is fixed before scoring, with no additions for deployment activity. The reasonableness check will show mismatches caused by this scope rather than by market fit. | 9 (validation); 12 (case study) | Accepted |
| A-002 | ASSUMPTION | The analytical market is the CBSA (Metropolitan Statistical Area), keyed by the 5-digit CBSA code (`market_id`), under the July 2023 OMB delineation. | CBSAs are official, built from counties, and published natively by Census and BLS. They also give a clean crosswalk for county-level data. | A CBSA does not match any operator's real service area. Early deployments cover small parts of a metro, and some metros share a single real-world market (for example, the Bay Area is split across the SF and San Jose CBSAs). | 5, 9 | Accepted |
| A-003 | LIMITATION | Sources come from different years. Waiting for the years to line up isn't possible. | Features from older sources (especially the EPA built-environment data) may understate recent change in fast-growing metros. Comparisons across features mix years. | 5 (EDA flags), 12 (methodology) | Accepted |

| A-007 | ASSUMPTION | The climate normals at the chosen station(s) are assumed to represent a CBSA's operating climate. The station-to-CBSA rule is not decided yet. | A-008 | LIMITATION | Metro-wide averages blur the difference between the urban core and the periphery. Raw MSA density is distorted by large, low-density outlying counties. | CBSAs include whole counties. Riverside–San Bernardino and Phoenix are examples where the county footprint is huge. | This could mis-rank markets on density-dependent features for robotaxis and sidewalk robots. The planned response is tract-level population-weighted density (a DERIVED measure). The formula is not chosen yet (data_sources.md §6). | 5, 6 | **Narrowed (Phase 5, recommendation pending approval).** Recommended D2, population-weighted tract density. Raw density has ρ 0.57 with D2 and understates Riverside ×26.6 and Las Vegas ×22.9. D3 block-group density (ρ 0.99) is the backup, and the ≥ 5,000/sq mi share (ρ 0.95) is a sensitivity alternative. Raw and central-county density are DROPped (mapping_methodology.md §1) |
| A-009 | ASSUMPTION | Airport activity is assigned to a CBSA only for FAA commercial-service airports located physically inside it (point-in-polygon on NASR coordinates). | This is a documented, reproducible rule that doesn't depend on unreliable county-name or city fields. | Metros served by airports just outside their boundary are under-counted. Examples: BWI for DC, ONT for LA, MHT/PVD for Boston. A "nearby hub" sensitivity variable is proposed, with its distance threshold still undecided. | 3, 6 | **Narrowed (Phase 5, recommendation pending approval).** Implemented as a NASR county name → 2023 county FIPS match (488 of 516 airports; the unmatched are outside every market), not point-in-polygon, because CBSA shapes are not in RAW (A-032). Recommended A1, inside-CBSA. BWI is inside Baltimore and ONT inside Riverside. MHT and ORH (for Boston) and SJC (for San Francisco) are outside every market; PVD is beyond 80 km. The sensitivity variant is A4, nearest-market within 80 km with no double counting (ρ 0.994). The A2 80 km radius is rejected because it double-counts BWI, ONT, SNA and DCA |
| A-010 | LIMITATION | Enplanements at connecting hubs (for example ATL, DFW, CLT, DEN, ORD) include connecting passengers who never enter the local market. | FAA enplanement data doesn't separate local passengers from connecting ones. | Airport demand is overstated for hub metros, including when expressed per capita. | 5, 6 | Accepted |

| A-012 | ASSUMPTION | For the 11 multi-state CBSAs, how state-level regulatory evidence maps to the market is undecided. | A-013 | LIMITATION | The EPA National Walkability Index is a *relative* national ranking (1–20). | A-014 | LIMITATION | Many candidate features are expected to rise with market size: population, households, establishments, enplanements, commuters. | Scale drives most count-based measures. | Size could be double-counted within or across components. | A-021 | ASSUMPTION | In the climate normals, a missing snow value is treated as **missing, not zero**.
